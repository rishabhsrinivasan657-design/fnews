When you buy a Treasury bond, you are lending money to the government in exchange for periodic interest payments and the return of your principal investment at maturity. These rising yields can signal investor concerns about inflation (the general rise in prices of goods and services over time) and expectations of future interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that controls interest rates). When investors believe that inflation will rise, they demand higher yields to compensate for the decreased purchasing power of their returns.

For everyday people, rising Treasury yields can have a direct impact on borrowing costs.
