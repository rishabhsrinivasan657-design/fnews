A Federal Reserve interest rate hike is a term used to describe the central bank's decision to increase the federal funds rate. The federal funds rate is the interest rate at which banks lend money to each other overnight. This rate is crucial because it influences borrowing costs for consumers and businesses. When the Federal Reserve raises this rate, it makes borrowing more expensive, which can slow down economic activity.

Interest rate hikes are often implemented to combat inflation (the general rise in prices of goods and services over time). When inflation is high, the Fed may decide to increase rates to make borrowing more expensive, thereby reducing spending and investment, which can help bring prices down.
