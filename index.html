The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) plays a critical role in shaping the U.S. economy through its management of monetary policy. As a component of the Federal Reserve, the FOMC is responsible for overseeing open market operations, which involve buying and selling government securities to influence the money supply and interest rates.

The committee meets regularly to assess economic conditions and make decisions regarding the federal funds rate, which is the interest rate at which banks lend money to each other overnight. This rate serves as a benchmark for other interest rates, including those for mortgages, car loans, and savings accounts. When the FOMC raises the federal funds rate, borrowing costs increase, which can slow down economic activity. Conversely, lowering the rate can stimulate growth by making borrowing cheaper.
