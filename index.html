When someone buys a government bond, they are essentially loaning money to the government in exchange for periodic interest payments and the return of the principal amount at maturity. The yield is the percentage return on this investment.

Recently, the 30-year government bond yield climbed to 5.70%, the highest level since 2002. This rise in yields can indicate that investors expect higher interest rates or inflation (the general rise in prices of goods and services over time). When bond yields increase, it often leads to higher borrowing costs for consumers, impacting loans, mortgages, and credit cards.

Higher borrowing costs can slow down economic growth, as consumers may be less likely to take out loans for big purchases like homes or cars. This can have a ripple effect on the economy, leading to reduced spending and investment.

Understanding government bond yields is essential for everyday people because they directly affect financial decisions.
