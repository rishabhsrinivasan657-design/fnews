The federal funds rate is a crucial concept in understanding how monetary policy affects the economy. It is the interest rate at which banks lend money to each other overnight. This rate is set by the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that controls interest rates) and serves as a benchmark for other interest rates in the economy.

When the Federal Reserve raises the federal funds rate, it typically leads to higher borrowing costs for consumers and businesses. This is because banks will pass on the increased costs to their customers in the form of higher interest rates on loans, mortgages, and credit cards.
