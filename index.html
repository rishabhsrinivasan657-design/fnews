Forward guidance is a concept that refers to how central banks communicate their future plans regarding monetary policy, which includes decisions about interest rates and other financial measures. By providing this information, central banks aim to influence market expectations and behavior. For example, if the Federal Reserve signals that it plans to raise interest rates, investors might adjust their strategies accordingly.

Recently, the Federal Reserve announced that it would no longer use forward guidance, shifting the focus to market-driven expectations. This change is significant because it means that investors will have to interpret economic data and signals on their own, potentially leading to increased market volatility (rapid changes in prices).

For everyday people, this shift can have real implications. When markets are volatile, it can affect everything from stock prices to the cost of loans and mortgages. If investors are uncertain about future interest rates, they may become more cautious, which can slow down economic growth.
